# What each addition cost

[Premise section 12](premise.md) wants adding a toggle to stay a small change of flow data, recordings and tests, and says to rethink the model if new toggles keep forcing engine changes. This is the record, kept apart from the premise, because the premise is loaded in every session and this is only needed when asking that question.

- **The first measurement is PKCE ([#10](https://github.com/vriesdemichael/authlines/issues/10)):** about 200 lines of flow data, settings, words and sources, 7 lines in the recording app, and tests. It did touch the engine three times, in small and general ways: a value can be held in memory rather than on a message, a pitfall's text can vary with the settings, and the heads of the lifelines now keep as much room as their chips need, where it used to be a fixed two rows. Its new chips also needed colours in two components, so those moved to one place and the next one needs none.
- **The second measurement is the attacker ([#26](https://github.com/vriesdemichael/authlines/issues/26)):** flow data, a new icon, a recording, no change to the model or the renderer, and every answer in the data matched a real server the first time it ran.
- **The third measurement is a refresh token ([#57](https://github.com/vriesdemichael/authlines/issues/57)):** flow data, a recording and a note, and one new kind of step, because time passing is not a message: the model only gained its name, and the renderer a way to draw it.
- **The fourth measurement is a second app ([#56](https://github.com/vriesdemichael/authlines/issues/56)):** flow data, recordings and notes, a new lifeline and chip, and no change to the model or the renderer. The recorder had to learn cookies and a second app of its own.
- **The fifth measurement is logging out ([#58](https://github.com/vriesdemichael/authlines/issues/58)):** flow data, recordings and notes, and one small addition to the model, because until now nothing that had been seen could stop existing: a step can end something, and the chips follow.
- The sixth measurement is a whole other flow, client credentials ([#14](https://github.com/vriesdemichael/authlines/issues/14)): flow data, recordings and notes, no change to the renderer, and to the model only why a setting is not on offer and what its first diagram is called. The screen needed a picker. The model held for a flow with no browser and no user, which step 0 had not tried.
- **The seventh is the device flow ([#14](https://github.com/vriesdemichael/authlines/issues/14)):** flow data, recordings, notes and three pictures, with nothing new in the model or the renderer, which already had a kind of step for people. Small changes went elsewhere: an address can come from a redirect as well as a request, and a word in search can open the flow it is about.
- The eighth measurement is the other ways the device flow ends: flow data, recordings and notes, and one small addition to the model, because this is the first setting that means something on one flow only. It says so, and no other flow has to test its combinations.
- The ninth measurement is two attacks on the device flow ([#33](https://github.com/vriesdemichael/authlines/issues/33)): flow data, recordings and notes, no change to the model or the renderer. The answers that stop one are the two other endings already there, so saying no is what changes the answer on the same arrow.
- The tenth measurement is a refused address: flow data, a recording and one small addition to the model, because until now a flow always went on to the end. A step can be where it stops.
- The eleventh measurement is an app that runs in the page ([#43](https://github.com/vriesdemichael/authlines/issues/43)): flow data, a recording from a real page, and two small additions to the model. A lifeline can join another, which moves its arrows and what they carry, and choosing something can bring other settings with it, because someone who knows where their app runs should not have to know that makes it a public client.
- The twelfth measurement is the old way ([#150](https://github.com/vriesdemichael/authlines/issues/150)): flow data, a recording from the demo app and API, and two small additions to the model. An intro can depend on the settings, and a setting can be one that only a question turns on, so it is a chip and never a control. The whole of what came before fits in as steps on the same flow, which takes the identity provider off the diagram.
- The thirteenth measurement is how the provider learns about the app ([#143](https://github.com/vriesdemichael/authlines/issues/143)): flow data, two recordings, and a recorder that can register a client and serve a document. Nothing new in the model or the renderer. It did show that listing the valid combinations multiplied with each setting, into millions of objects, which took most of the time the unit tests took. Walking between valid ones took the unit tests from 17 seconds to 7.
- The fourteenth measurement is users in a directory ([#144](https://github.com/vriesdemichael/authlines/issues/144)): flow data, a recording, a note, and a recorder that can run an LDAP server and read its log. Two small additions elsewhere: a switch can bring another setting along, as a choice's option already could, so that it and another provider turn each other off, and a recorded message can be the lines of a log. The diagram's renderer did not change, and the raw view has one line of its own for a log.
- The fifteenth measurement is an app in the page with a server behind it ([#145](https://github.com/vriesdemichael/authlines/issues/145)): flow data, a recording from a real page and a small server of the demo app's own, which the recorder also asks what a script in the page can do, and one small addition to the settings: an option can give its own reason, because the setting's reason says the tokens end up in the browser, and this option is there to stop that. Nothing new in the model or the renderer. The rail in simple mode has a word budget, and the new label did not fit, so the other two got shorter.
- The sixteenth measurement is customers with providers of their own, and SAML ([#118](https://github.com/vriesdemichael/authlines/issues/118)): flow data, two recordings with two realms standing in for customers, questions the recorder asks, a note, and a recorder that can set up organizations and a SAML provider. Two small additions to the model: what a person sees at a step can depend on the settings, as its words could, and a page that is not a redirect is at the address of the request it answered. The raw view learned to open a SAML message, as it opens a token. The renderer did not change: SAML's form posts are arrows through the browser like any other.
- The seventeenth measurement is someone leaving a customer's company ([#118](https://github.com/vriesdemichael/authlines/issues/118)): flow data, two recordings, questions the recorder asks in a real browser, and no change to the model or the renderer. The demo app gained a hook for what happens while someone is logged in, and the recorder a way to draw a second login as steps of their own.
