For the agent

The rest of this page is what the skill tells an agent, and it is also one Markdown file.

Authlines draws OAuth and OpenID Connect flows as sequence diagrams, recorded from a real Keycloak 26.8.1. If you are an agent helping someone with their own system, you can link to the flow and settings that match it, open it on the step you are explaining, and put their names on the parties. They get a diagram to step through, with who has seen what, and the requests a real server sent.

Nothing here contacts their system. You describe it with a link, and the site draws its own recording with their names on it.

The address

https://vriesdemichael.github.io/authlines/<flow>/[setup/]?<settings and view>#v=1&<party>=<name>

Leave out a setting to keep its default. The flow, its settings and the step are in the query, so the link opens exactly that. A setting that does not fit with the others is turned off, and the screen says why. A step these settings do not draw opens the nearest one before it that they do, and with no step it opens at the start.

What is not drawn here

Authlines does not draw apps on a phone, a PKCE downgrade, passkeys and WebAuthn, Kerberos, NTLM, or client certificates.

A login the other provider hands on again, to a provider behind it, is drawn as one hop, and broker=1&step=upstream-authorize says why.

Names for the parties

  • They go after #, start with v=1, and name parties by the ids listed for each flow: #v=1&app=Payroll&idp=Entra%20ID.
  • They change the names on the heads of the diagram and nothing else. Every request and answer shown stays the one recorded on the site’s own server, and the raw view says which recorded value is which named party.
  • A name is plain text of at most 32 characters, in any language. No < or >, no address, nothing that looks like a token, and no characters that do not show, such as one that only changes the direction of text.
  • Encode each name as a value in a query string: %20 or + for a space, %26 for &, %23 for #, and other letters as UTF-8.
  • The part after # stays in the browser and never reaches a server. Anyone the link is shared with sees the names, so never put a secret in one.
  • A party the flow has, but that these settings do not draw, is simply not shown. A party the flow does not have, or a name that does not fit, is left out and said, beside the diagram.

Examples

  • A web app on a server, logging people in with OpenID Connect and PKCE, at a provider that is not Keycloak.

  • The same app, where the identity provider sends the code back through the browser, in advanced mode.
    https://vriesdemichael.github.io/authlines/authorization-code/?mode=advanced&oidc=1&pkce=1&keycloak=off&step=redirect-with-code#v=1&app=Payroll&idp=Entra%20ID&api=HR%20API
  • How that app gets set up with its provider.
    https://vriesdemichael.github.io/authlines/authorization-code/setup/?oidc=1&pkce=1&keycloak=off#v=1&app=Payroll&idp=Entra%20ID&app-team=Payroll%20team&idp-admin=IT
  • A job with no user, calling an API with a token of its own.
    https://vriesdemichael.github.io/authlines/client-credentials/#v=1&app=Nightly%20export&api=Billing%20API
  • A television app where the person logs in on their phone.
    https://vriesdemichael.github.io/authlines/device/#v=1&app=Living%20room%20TV&browser=Their%20phone
  • A link with no version, which draws the site’s own names and says why.
    https://vriesdemichael.github.io/authlines/authorization-code/#app=Payroll

How it is shown

  • mode=advanced: Raw requests and answers, which spec says so, and server notes.
  • step=<id>: The step to open on, by the ids listed for each diagram.
  • say=specs: The specs’ names for the parties. say=keycloak for Keycloak’s.
  • keycloak=off: No notes about Keycloak. Use this when the person’s identity provider is something else, and give it its name with idp=. When only the provider it hands logins to is something else, keep the notes and name that one with upstream=. The traffic shown is still the site’s own Keycloak’s, and the raw view says so.
  • eyes=<party>: The whole flow as one party saw it, such as eyes=browser, for a question about what one party can see. It works with step=.
  • whole=1: Every step at once, for a room.

Authorization code flow: authorization-code

An app gets access on behalf of a user, without ever seeing their password.

Settings

Each value says what it needs with it, and what it can share a link with, worked out from the rules the screen uses, two settings at a time. A link that names two that do not go together loses one of them.

  • oidc=1: OpenID Connect, off by default. Adds proof of who logged in on top of the access OAuth grants. oidc=1 does not go with register=dcr, thief=1, checks=bearer, directory=1, companies=1, speaks=saml, leaves=1, send=none, send=expired, send=changed, send=elsewhere, send=noscope, link=1, linkends=cancelled, or runs=bff.
  • consent=1: Ask for consent, off by default. The identity provider asks the user whether the app may have this access. consent=1 goes only with oidc=1, client=public, register=cimd, pkce=1, par=1, attacker=1, uses=real-app, binding=dpop, nonce=1, broker=1, upstream=v1, upstream=v2, reads=api, reads=refused, exchange=1, trade=refresh, refresh=1, and reuse=ends among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • client=confidential | public: Client type, confidential by default. Whether the app can keep a secret. One that cannot, such as an app in a browser, has to rely on PKCE. client=public does not go with register=dcr, thief=1, checks=bearer, uses=real-app, directory=1, companies=1, speaks=saml, leaves=1, link=1, linkends=cancelled, or runs=bff.client=public goes with upstream=v2 only with reads=api or reads=refused as well.
  • register=manual | dcr | cimd: How the provider learns about the app, manual by default. Someone registers it by hand, it registers itself, or it points at a document about itself. It changes the setup, and with a document the login too. register=cimd needs consent=1&client=public&pkce=1 with it.register=dcr goes with none of the other settings yet, so leave them out.register=cimd goes only with oidc=1, consent=1, client=public, pkce=1, and broker=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • pkce=1: PKCE, off by default. The app proves it started this login, so a stolen code is no use alone. Current advice is to have it on, and a public client must. pkce=1 does not go with register=dcr, thief=1, checks=bearer, directory=1, companies=1, speaks=saml, leaves=1, link=1, or linkends=cancelled.
  • par=1: PAR, off by default. The app sends its request straight to the identity provider, and the browser carries only the client ID and a reference. Drawn so far with nothing else on but OpenID Connect, consent and PKCE, with either kind of client and the app on a server. par=1 goes only with oidc=1, consent=1, client=public, and pkce=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • attacker=1: An attacker, off by default. Someone gets hold of the code and tries to use it. Flip PKCE, the client type or how they get it to see what changes. The attacker only shows the plain case, with a Bearer token and no other provider. attacker=1 goes only with oidc=1, consent=1, client=public, pkce=1, uses=real-app, gets=link, and registered=any among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • thief=1: Show someone using a stolen token, off by default. Someone has the access token, and calls the API with it, without a proof. Only with a DPoP token, in the plain case so far. thief=1 needs binding=dpop with it.thief=1 goes only with checks=bearer and binding=dpop among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • checks=proof | bearer: What the API checks, proof by default. An API that checks the DPoP proof, or one written before DPoP that knows only Bearer. Only someone using a stolen token asks this. checks=bearer needs thief=1&binding=dpop with it.checks=bearer goes only with thief=1 and binding=dpop among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • uses=themselves | real-app: What they do with it, themselves by default. A secret stops them trading the code themselves. They can also get the real app to trade it for them, secret and all. Only an app with a secret needs this, and only for a copied code. With no secret, they can just trade it. uses=real-app needs attacker=1 with it.uses=real-app goes only with oidc=1, consent=1, pkce=1, and attacker=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • gets=copy | link: How they get the code, copy by default. They copy it as it goes by, or they get the user to open a login link of their own, which asks for the code to go to them. Only in the plain case so far, with the app on a server. gets=link needs attacker=1 with it.gets=link goes only with oidc=1, client=public, pkce=1, attacker=1, and registered=any among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • registered=exact | any: What the app registered, exact by default. Its exact address, or *, which some put there to make a redirect_uri error go away. Only a link of theirs asks this. registered=any needs attacker=1&gets=link with it.registered=any goes only with oidc=1, client=public, pkce=1, attacker=1, and gets=link among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • binding=bearer | dpop: Token type, bearer by default. Whether holding the token is enough, or the app must also prove it holds a key. binding=dpop goes only with oidc=1, consent=1, client=public, pkce=1, thief=1, checks=bearer, nonce=1, broker=1, and upstream=v1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • nonce=1: Server requires a nonce, off by default. The server rejects the first proof, to limit how long a proof can be used. A DPoP nonce only applies to DPoP tokens. nonce=1 needs binding=dpop with it.nonce=1 goes only with oidc=1, consent=1, client=public, pkce=1, binding=dpop, broker=1, and upstream=v1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • broker=1: External IdP, off by default. The IdP hands the login to another IdP. The app does not notice. broker=1 goes only with oidc=1, consent=1, client=public, register=cimd, pkce=1, binding=dpop, nonce=1, companies=1, speaks=saml, leaves=1, upstream=v1, upstream=v2, reads=api, reads=refused, exchange=1, refresh=1, and sso=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • directory=1: Users in a directory, off by default. The identity provider checks users in LDAP or Active Directory. Only drawn in the plain case so far. directory=1 goes with none of the other settings yet, so leave them out.
  • companies=1: Each customer has a provider, off by default. Users log in at their own company, which the identity provider finds from their email. Only with another provider, in the plain case so far. companies=1 needs broker=1 with it.companies=1 goes only with broker=1, speaks=saml, and leaves=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • speaks=oidc | saml: What their provider speaks, oidc by default. OpenID Connect, as on the rest of this diagram, or SAML, which many companies still run. Only a customer's own provider is asked this. speaks=saml needs broker=1&companies=1 with it.speaks=saml goes only with broker=1, companies=1, and leaves=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • leaves=1: Someone leaves the company, off by default. Their company turns their account off, and what still works here. Only someone at a customer with a provider of their own leaves it. leaves=1 needs broker=1&companies=1 with it.leaves=1 goes only with broker=1, companies=1, and speaks=saml among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • upstream=off | v1 | v2: Stored upstream tokens, off by default. The IdP keeps the upstream IdP's tokens, and the app can ask for them. Upstream tokens only exist when another IdP handles the login. upstream=v1 needs broker=1 with it.upstream=v2 needs broker=1 with it.upstream=v1 goes only with oidc=1, consent=1, client=public, pkce=1, binding=dpop, nonce=1, and broker=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.upstream=v2 goes only with oidc=1, consent=1, pkce=1, broker=1, reads=api, and reads=refused among the other settings, so leave the rest out.upstream=v2 goes with client=public only with reads=api or reads=refused as well.
  • reads=app | api | refused: Who reads it, app by default. The app that logged in, or an API it calls, with a client of its own that the identity provider allows this provider. This is drawn with API V2, where whoever reads the token proves who it is. reads=api needs broker=1&upstream=v2 with it.reads=refused needs broker=1&upstream=v2 with it.reads=api goes only with oidc=1, consent=1, client=public, pkce=1, broker=1, and upstream=v2 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.reads=refused goes only with oidc=1, consent=1, client=public, pkce=1, broker=1, and upstream=v2 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • send=good | none | expired | changed | elsewhere | noscope: What the API is sent, good by default. The same call to the API, and what it answers depending on the token. This shows the plain case, so other settings do not get in the way of the answer. send=none goes only with client=public and pkce=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.send=expired goes only with client=public and pkce=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.send=changed goes only with client=public and pkce=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.send=elsewhere goes only with client=public and pkce=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.send=noscope goes only with client=public and pkce=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • exchange=1: Token exchange, off by default. The API needs a second API to answer, and trades the token it got for one meant for that API. Drawn so far with nothing else on but OpenID Connect, consent, PKCE and another provider, with either kind of client and the app on a server. exchange=1 goes only with oidc=1, consent=1, client=public, pkce=1, broker=1, and trade=refresh among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • trade=token | refresh: What the trade gives back, token by default. Only a token for the second API, or a refresh token as well, so that the API can get another later without the user. There is nothing to trade until token exchange is on. trade=refresh needs exchange=1 with it.trade=refresh goes only with oidc=1, consent=1, client=public, pkce=1, and exchange=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • link=1: Account linking, off by default. Logged in, the user links an account at another provider, whose tokens the identity provider can keep. Drawn so far only on the plain flow, for an app with a secret and nothing else on. link=1 goes only with linkends=cancelled among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • linkends=linked | cancelled: How the link ends, linked by default. The user goes on and links it, or cancels at the page that asks. There is no link to end until account linking is on. linkends=cancelled needs link=1 with it.linkends=cancelled goes only with link=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • refresh=1: When the token runs out, off by default. Later on the access token expires, and the app gets a new one without the user. This shows the plain case, so other settings do not get in the way of the answer. refresh=1 goes only with oidc=1, consent=1, client=public, pkce=1, broker=1, and reuse=ends among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • reuse=keeps | ends: Using a refresh token twice, keeps by default. Whether an old refresh token still works once a new one has come back. There is no second use until the token runs out and the first has happened. reuse=ends needs refresh=1 with it.reuse=ends goes only with oidc=1, consent=1, client=public, pkce=1, and refresh=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • sso=1: A second app, off by default. A second app, the same identity provider, and no password. This shows the plain case, so other settings do not get in the way of the answer. sso=1 goes only with oidc=1, client=public, pkce=1, broker=1, second=insists, second=fresh, logout=1, and keeps=offline among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • second=remembered | insists | fresh: What happens at the second app, remembered by default. Whether the identity provider goes by the session, and what happens with none. There is no second app until it has been brought on. second=insists needs oidc=1&sso=1 with it.second=fresh needs oidc=1&sso=1 with it.second=insists goes only with oidc=1, client=public, pkce=1, and sso=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.second=fresh goes only with oidc=1, client=public, pkce=1, and sso=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • logout=1: Log out, off by default. The user logs out, and what ends is not everything. Part of OpenID Connect. logout=1 needs oidc=1 with it.logout=1 goes only with oidc=1, client=public, pkce=1, sso=1, and keeps=offline among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • keeps=refresh | offline: What the app keeps, refresh by default. A refresh token, or an offline token, which a service can use when the user is not there. There is no session to outlive until the user logs out. keeps=offline needs oidc=1&logout=1 with it.keeps=offline goes only with oidc=1, client=public, pkce=1, sso=1, and logout=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • runs=server | page | bff | program: Where the app runs, server by default. On a server of its own, in the page in the user’s browser, alone or with a server behind it, or in a program on the user’s computer. In the page alone there is nowhere to keep a secret, and in a program a secret shipped in every copy is no secret. runs=page needs client=public with it.runs=bff needs pkce=1 with it.runs=program needs client=public&pkce=1 with it.runs=page goes only with oidc=1, client=public, and pkce=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.runs=bff goes only with pkce=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.runs=program goes only with oidc=1, client=public, and pkce=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
  • address=registered | other: The address the app asks for, registered by default. Where the user should be sent back to, and what happens when that is not an address registered for this app. It stops before any of those would matter. address=other goes only with oidc=1, client=public, and pkce=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.

Parties:

  • browser: User's browser, where the user is. With runs=page it is drawn as Browser and app. With runs=bff it is drawn as Browser and page.
  • app: App, what the user uses. Not with runs=page. With runs=bff it is drawn as App’s server. With runs=program it is drawn as Program.
  • second-app: Second app, same provider. Only with sso=1.
  • idp: Identity provider, logs users in.
  • attacker: Someone else, has no business here. Only with attacker=1.
  • directory: User directory, has the users. Only with directory=1.
  • upstream: Other provider, the other account. Only with broker=1 or link=1. With companies=1 or speaks=saml or leaves=1 it is drawn as Their company.
  • api: API, has the data.
  • second-api: Second API, the api calls it. Only with exchange=1.

Device flow: device

An app with no good way to log anyone in, and a user who does that on another device.

Settings

Each value says what it needs with it, and what it can share a link with, worked out from the rules the screen uses, two settings at a time. A link that names two that do not go together loses one of them.

  • oidc=1: OpenID Connect, off by default. Adds proof of who logged in on top of the access OAuth grants.
  • ends=agrees | says-no | runs-out: How it ends, agrees by default. Whether the user agrees, says no, or never comes. The app only finds out from the answer the next time it asks. ends=says-no does not go with misuse=reads-code.ends=runs-out does not go with misuse=reads-code.
  • misuse=nobody | sends-code | reads-code: Someone misusing it, nobody by default. Someone starts this on a device of their own and sends the user the code, or reads the user code off a screen and gets there first. No password gets stolen in either. misuse=reads-code goes only with oidc=1 among the other settings, so leave the rest out.
